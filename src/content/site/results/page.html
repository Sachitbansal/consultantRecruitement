What results has GOP delivered? GOP's practice leads have delivered the following anonymised results in earlier engagements across manufacturing: capacity doubled in heat exchangers, changeover cut by 45% in a press shop, throughput up 50% in fabrication, and manpower optimised by 32% in a new fan plant.

These results were delivered by the senior practitioners who now lead GOP's manufacturing and supply chain practice. Client names are withheld by agreement, and we describe each client by type.

Heat exchangers: capacity doubled

Installed capacity doubled through takt, flow and pull implementation, with no additional floor space.

Press shop: changeover from 70 to 40 minutes

Changeover time was cut from 70 to 40 minutes, a 45% reduction, directly raising available production hours.

Fabrication: throughput up 50%, capacity doubled

Throughput rose 50% and productivity 60%. Capacity doubled from 50 to 100 units a month. Manpower fell by 25% and the footprint became 40% smaller.

Fan manufacturer, new plant: 6 lakh units a month

Manpower was optimised by 32% (from 817 to 558), and the plant was built in 40% less space than conventional design.

Materials handling redesign: 60% manpower optimisation

A plant was brought live with 60% manpower optimisation through conveyorised flow.

The methods behind these results

Where the method behind a result is recorded, it is:

  • Heat exchangers: takt, flow and pull implementation, so work is pulled through the plant instead of pushed in batches.
  • Press shop: changeover reduction, separating the work that needs the press stopped from the work that does not, then standardising the routine.
  • New fan plant: an optimised plant design, compared with a conventional design for the same volume.
  • Materials handling: conveyorised flow in place of manual movement between operations.

The method for the first two is set out in how manufacturers can double capacity without new floor space and how to cut changeover time.

How to read anyone's results, including ours

Before you rely on any consultant's results, ask a few questions. Was the figure measured the same way before and after, on the same definition? Over what period was it measured, and has it held since? What was the starting condition, and how similar is it to your business? Who did the work: the people who would work on your business, or someone else? A good firm will answer these plainly. In GOP's own engagements, baselines are set during the diagnostic and agreed with the client's team, and progress is tracked weekly on a shared scoreboard, so these questions have answers from the start.
