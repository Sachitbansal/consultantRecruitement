Use Digital and AI to Improve Business Performance.

Technology should solve a business problem. GOP helps identify opportunities where digital capabilities, automation, data and AI can support growth, productivity and better decisions.

By GOP Editorial · Published 24 October 2026 · Updated 24 October 2026

Where can digital tools and AI create practical value in a mid-size business? In the decisions and hand-offs that cost the business money every week: quoting, lead follow-up, receivable chasing, planning, exception alerts and month-end reporting. GOP starts with the business objective, pilots a practical solution on real data and keeps it only if it measurably improves the work.

The problem this lever solves

Most promoter-led businesses have bought software that is used for a fraction of what it can do, and run the rest of the business on spreadsheets, phone calls and memory. AI adds a new layer of promise and confusion. The risk is spending on tools before deciding which decision or process they are meant to improve.

Digital and AI work at GOP is not a separate technology offering. It supports the other five levers, and every use case is tied to a measure one of those levers already tracks.

Potential focus areas

Digital demand generation, and search and AI-search visibility

Answer-first pages, structured data and consistent descriptions across the web, so buyers and AI assistants can find and describe the business accurately. See how B2B manufacturers get found in AI search.

Sales and marketing automation, and customer and market intelligence

Account research, prospect scoring, outreach drafting and follow-up reminders that keep every lead moving, plus quote support and margin alerts at order entry.

Workflow automation and process improvement

Removing manual hand-offs and re-keying between sales, planning, stores, dispatch and accounts, and automating receivable follow-up.

Management dashboards

A small set of measures, drawn automatically from the systems you already have, that the promoter and leadership read every week instead of waiting for month-end.

AI-assisted knowledge and analysis, and technology-enabled operating models

Using AI to summarise documents, draft first versions of routine analysis and surface exceptions in planning, inventory and supplier data, with people responsible for every decision. Where it fits, digital service delivery and new operating models built around the data the business already holds.

How we work

Start with the business objective, identify the process or decision to improve, assess the data and technology requirements, pilot a practical solution, and measure whether it delivers value.

  1. Name the decision or process to improve, and its current measure.
  2. Check the data: where it lives, how clean it is and who owns it.
  3. Assess risk, cost and the capacity to implement.
  4. Pilot on real work for a fixed period.
  5. Keep, change or stop the pilot based on the measure.

We do not recommend AI transformation without an assessment of data, systems, risk and implementation capacity.

How progress is measured

  • Time from enquiry to quote
  • Share of leads with a follow-up on time
  • Days sales outstanding
  • Hours spent on manual reporting each month
  • Adoption: share of the team using the tool for the intended task

Who this lever is for

Businesses where quoting, follow-up, planning or reporting still depends on spreadsheets and individual memory, and leadership teams that want to know where AI is worth trying before committing money to it. It also suits portfolio companies whose value-creation plan includes productivity or reporting improvements that data and automation can speed up.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying a tool before naming the decision it should improve.
  • Automating a broken process.
  • Running a pilot without a before-and-after measure.
  • Leaving data ownership undefined.

Questions to ask yourself before starting

  • Which recurring decision costs us the most when it is late or wrong?
  • Do sales, finance and operations work from the same customer and product data?
  • Who would own a new tool after the pilot ends?

A hypothetical scenario

Hypothetical, for illustration only. Not a client case. A distributor-led manufacturer takes three days to turn an enquiry into a quote, and loses orders to faster competitors. The team maps the quoting steps, finds most of the time is spent pulling prices and stock from separate sheets, and pilots a simple quote assistant on live enquiries for six weeks. Quote time is measured before and after. The tool is kept for the product lines where it helped and dropped where it did not.

Frequently asked questions

Does every business need AI?

No. Technology should be selected based on business value, feasibility, risk and organisational readiness.

Can GOP help identify automation opportunities?

The engagement can assess processes for repetitive work, manual hand-offs, information gaps and other opportunities for improvement.

Will AI automatically reduce costs?

No. Benefits depend on implementation, adoption, data quality and process redesign.

Do we need new software to start?

Usually not. Most pilots start with the systems and data the business already has.
