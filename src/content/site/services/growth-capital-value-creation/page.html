Prepare for Capital. Build Value After It Arrives.

Capital can accelerate growth when the business is clear about its priorities, deployment plan and operating requirements. GOP helps businesses strengthen their preparation, raise growth capital and execute against agreed growth objectives.

By GOP Editorial · Published 24 October 2026 · Updated 24 October 2026

How does GOP help a business raise growth capital? GOP does not invest its own money. It helps a promoter-led business build the case for capital, prepare the pitch deck and investor information, take the pitch to suitable mid-market PE and VC investors and work through the raise. After investment, GOP helps leadership turn the investment thesis into a plan and deliver it.

The problem this lever solves

Promoters often approach a raise as a document exercise: a deck, a valuation expectation and a list of names. Investors look for something else: a clear reason for capital, a deployment plan that shows how money becomes earnings, numbers that reconcile, and a team that can deliver without the promoter in every room.

After the money arrives, a second gap opens. The investment thesis assumed growth, margin and capability changes that now have to happen on a timetable, inside an organisation that has not changed yet. Many businesses lose the first year to that gap.

Before investment

  • Clarify the business case for capital.
  • Establish growth priorities and intended capital deployment.
  • Strengthen management information.
  • Identify key business and execution risks.
  • Improve the quality of the growth plan.
  • Prepare for relevant investor discussions.

Raising growth capital

The investment case and pitch deck

We help the leadership team write the investment case in plain terms: where the business is, where capital takes it, how the money is deployed and what the investor should expect to see. The pitch deck follows from that case, not the other way round.

Investor information and data room

We prepare the operating and financial information investors will ask for, consistent with the company's audited accounts and reviewed by its own finance team and auditors, and organise it before the first meeting.

Taking the pitch to investors

Within the agreed mandate, we help identify suitable mid-market PE and VC investors for the business's size, sector and stage, prepare the promoter for the pitch and support the meetings.

Through diligence

We help the business answer diligence questions quickly and consistently, working alongside the legal, tax and financial advisers the company appoints for the transaction documents.

After investment

  • Translate the investment thesis into an operating plan.
  • Prioritise value-creation initiatives.
  • Clarify leadership responsibilities.
  • Establish progress reviews and agreed measures.
  • Identify implementation bottlenecks.
  • Track and assess realised improvements.

The first 100 days set the rhythm: a value-creation plan with owners, a scoreboard the investor and the board both read, and a review cadence that surfaces problems early. See a 90-day transformation plan.

How progress is measured

  • Readiness gaps closed before the first investor meeting
  • Time to answer diligence requests
  • Delivery of the value-creation plan against milestones
  • Agreed operating measures in the investment thesis
  • Board and investor reporting on time and consistent

Who this lever is for

Promoter-led businesses, typically in the INR 150-500 crore revenue range, with a clear use for growth capital such as new capacity, new markets or acquisitions, and leadership willing to work to an investor's standards. After investment, it suits PE/VC funds and their portfolio companies that need the thesis turned into a plan and delivered.

How GOP works on this lever

GOP works as operators alongside the promoter, not as external advisors. Every engagement starts with a fixed-scope diagnostic, moves to a sequenced roadmap with owners, and then to implementation inside your team against agreed measures. See our approach, or read about the other capabilities.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Building the deck before the investment case.
  • Approaching investors before the numbers reconcile.
  • Treating the raise as the finish line.
  • Leaving the post-investment plan until the investor asks for it.

A hypothetical scenario

Hypothetical, for illustration only. Not a client case. A promoter-led manufacturer wants capital for a new line and a move into exports. The first draft deck leads with valuation. The team rewrites the investment case around the deployment plan and the margin evidence, fixes customer-level reporting, and prepares a data room. The pitch goes to a short list of mid-market funds that fit the business's size and sector. After investment, the same plan becomes the 100-day agenda the board reviews each month.

Frequently asked questions

Does GOP invest its own capital?

No. GOP does not invest its own money in the businesses it works with.

Can GOP introduce investors?

Yes, within an agreed mandate. GOP helps prepare the pitch and takes it to suitable mid-market PE and VC investors. It does not guarantee investor interest, access or a successful raise.

Does GOP guarantee post-investment performance?

No. GOP can support execution and measurement, but business outcomes depend on many factors.

Who handles the legal and regulatory side of a raise?

The company's own legal, tax and financial advisers, and any registered intermediary the transaction requires. GOP works alongside them on the business and operating side.
