Governance and MIS a mid-size manufacturer needs

By GOP Editorial · Published 23 October 2026 · Updated 23 October 2026 · 4 min read

Governance and MIS give owners, lenders and investors confidence that the numbers are right and risks are watched. A manufacturer with INR 150-500 crore revenue needs both before it raises capital or lists: a board that meets on a calendar with clear approval limits, and a short monthly MIS pack that arrives on time and reconciles with the accounts.

Governance essentials

  • A board with independent perspective, meeting on a fixed calendar
  • A defined delegation of authority and approval limits
  • Policies for related-party transactions, risk and compliance
  • A clear separation between promoter, board and management roles

MIS essentials

A monthly MIS pack should reach leaders within a few days of month-end and cover:

  • Profit and loss by product, customer and plant
  • Working capital: receivables, inventory and payables
  • Order book and pipeline
  • Operations KPIs: capacity, quality and delivery
  • Variance against budget with owners and actions

How to start

Agree the ten to fifteen metrics the board will see, assign an owner to each and run one reporting cycle before adding more. A small pack that is accurate and on time earns more trust than a large pack that is late and disputed.

A monthly rhythm that works

Governance and MIS succeed or fail on rhythm more than design. A pattern that suits many mid-size manufacturers: the books close in the first few working days; the finance head circulates the MIS pack shortly after; each metric owner adds a line on variance and action; the management review takes the pack in week two; and the board sees a quarterly version with the same structure. When every meeting starts from the same pack, arguments move from whose numbers are right to what to do about them.

Keep a simple action log alongside the pack. Each review should close with owners and dates, and the next review should open by checking them.

Why this matters for investors

Investors read MIS to understand how the business really performs and how well management understands it. Governance shows whether the promoter, board and management each play their role. Both are checked early in due diligence.

Common mistakes

  • Producing a large MIS pack that arrives after decisions are made.
  • Letting related-party transactions go undocumented.
  • Holding board meetings without papers circulated in advance.
  • Mixing promoter, board and management roles so accountability is unclear.

A checklist you can use this week

  1. Choose ten to fifteen board metrics and name a business owner for each.
  2. Fix a monthly calendar for MIS delivery and board meetings.
  3. List all related-party transactions and the policy that governs them.
  4. Write down the roles of promoter, board and management in a page.
  5. Run one full reporting cycle before adding any new report.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need independent directors before an investment?

Not always, but an independent voice on the board strengthens governance and is often welcomed by investors. Requirements depend on company type and the deal, so seek legal advice.

What is a good size for an MIS pack?

Start with ten to fifteen metrics and one or two pages. Add detail only when someone uses it to make decisions.

Who should own MIS?

The finance head typically owns production of the pack, while each metric has a named business owner who explains and acts on it.
