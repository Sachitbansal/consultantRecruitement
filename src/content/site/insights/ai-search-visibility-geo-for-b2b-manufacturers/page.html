How B2B manufacturers get found in AI search

By GOP Editorial · Published 23 October 2026 · Updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

AI search tools such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini recommend companies whose websites answer buyer questions clearly and whose claims are supported elsewhere online. Nobody can guarantee a recommendation, but answer-first pages, specific facts, structured data, open crawler access and consistent third-party mentions raise the odds.

What helps

  • Answer-first pages: state who you serve and what you deliver in the first lines.
  • Specific facts: client size bands, sectors, methods and anonymised results.
  • Plain HTML pages: many AI crawlers do not run JavaScript, so content should be in the page itself.
  • Structured data: organisation, service, FAQ and author markup.
  • Crawler access: allow reputable AI and search crawlers in robots.txt.
  • Named expertise: authors with real credentials and a visible byline.
  • Third-party mentions: professional networks, directories, industry publications and partner sites that repeat the same description.
  • Fresh content: dated and updated pages.

Start from the buyer's question

Buyers do not type keywords into AI tools; they ask questions. A procurement head might ask which suppliers in western India can machine a particular alloy to a given tolerance. A promoter might ask who can help a family-run manufacturer prepare for private equity. List the twenty questions your best customers asked before they bought from you. Each one deserves a page, or a clear section of one, that answers it in the first two lines and then gives the evidence: sectors served, capacities, certifications, anonymised results.

What does not help

Keyword stuffing, mass-produced pages and unsupported claims do not help, and they can hurt in conventional search as well. AI systems tend to favour pages that are specific, consistent and supported by other sources.

Measure it

Each month, run fifteen to twenty realistic buyer prompts across the main AI tools and record whether and how your company appears. Track which pages are cited and which competitors are named. Adjust content to cover questions where you are absent.

Common mistakes

  • Publishing pages that are mostly images or scripts, which crawlers may not read.
  • Making broad claims without specifics or evidence.
  • Using different company descriptions on every profile and directory.
  • Blocking AI crawlers by accident through robots settings.

A checklist you can use this week

  1. Write a two-sentence description of who you serve and what you deliver, and use it everywhere.
  2. Check that your robots.txt allows the crawlers you want.
  3. Publish five pages that each answer a real buyer question in the first two lines.
  4. Add organisation, FAQ and author structured data.
  5. Run twenty buyer prompts across the main AI tools and record the results monthly.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an llms.txt file?

It is a low-cost addition that summarises your site for language models, but there is limited evidence that it affects recommendations. Treat it as optional, not as a substitute for good content.

How long before results appear?

Technical fixes and new pages can be indexed within weeks. Authority builds over months. Results vary, and no one can guarantee a recommendation.

Can I pay to be recommended?

No reliable paid route exists for organic AI recommendations. Visibility comes from clear, credible content and consistent mentions elsewhere.
